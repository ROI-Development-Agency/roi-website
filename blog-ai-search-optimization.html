Search & SEO

Getting found by the AI.

Search & SEO 7 min read By ROI Development Agency

More and more of your customers aren't scrolling a page of blue links anymore. They ask ChatGPT, they read Google's AI Overview at the top of the results, they let Gemini or Perplexity summarise their options — and they act on whatever those answers say. If your business isn't inside that answer, you're invisible to a fast-growing slice of your market, no matter how well you "rank."

This is the shift every business owner needs to understand right now. Search didn't just get an AI feature bolted on — the destination changed. Here's what's happening and how to make sure your brand is the one the AI quotes.

Ranking #1 is no longer the finish line

For twenty years, the game was simple: get to the top of Google's results and the clicks follow. That game is being rewritten. When someone asks a question now, an AI often answers it directly — citing a handful of sources — before the traditional links ever get a look.

And here's the uncomfortable part: the pages Google ranks first are frequently not the ones the AI chooses to quote. Across recent analyses, the overlap between the top traditional search results and the sources AI engines actually cite has fallen from roughly 70% to under 20%. In other words, you can be #1 on Google and still be absent from the answer your customer reads.

Meet GEO — optimising to be the answer

The discipline that fixes this has a name: Generative Engine Optimization (GEO), sometimes called Answer Engine Optimization. If classic SEO is about ranking a link, GEO is about becoming part of the synthesised answer itself — the sentence the AI reads back to your customer, with your brand attached as the source.

It's not a replacement for SEO. It's the next layer on top of it. In fact, the domains that already rank well tend to be the ones AI cites most — so strong SEO foundations are the price of entry, not an alternative.

How to get your business cited by AI

The good news: the moves that win AI citations are things a well-run content strategy should be doing anyway. The difference is doing them deliberately.

  • Front-load the answer. Lead each page with a clear, direct answer to the question in the first 80–120 words — that's the chunk AI models paraphrase. Bury your point three paragraphs down and you won't get quoted.
  • Write in clean, structured blocks. Clear headings, short answers, and question-and-answer formatting. The same patterns that won Google's "featured snippets" a few years ago are exactly what wins AI citations today.
  • Add structured data (schema). FAQPage, HowTo, and Article schema help AI engines understand and trust your content — and it's used as a signal when they pick who to cite.
  • Prove real expertise (E-E-A-T). Genuine experience, named authors, and trustworthy, well-sourced content. AI systems are trained to prefer sources that demonstrate authority, not just keyword-stuffed pages.
  • Let the AI crawlers in. If your site blocks the bots that feed ChatGPT, Perplexity, and Google's AI, you've opted out of the whole game without realising it.
Ranking #1 used to be the finish line. Now the finish line is being the sentence the AI reads back to your customer.

Why this is worth doing now

Two reasons. First, the upside is real: analysis from Seer Interactive found that brands cited in AI answers earn dramatically more clicks — both organic and paid — than competitors who aren't cited. When the AI names you as the answer, you inherit its authority.

Second, it's a land-grab. Most businesses in your space haven't adjusted yet — they're still optimising for a results page fewer people scroll. The brands that move now become the "default answer" for their category, and that position is hard for a latecomer to dislodge. It's the same logic we walk through in SEO vs paid ads: build the asset that compounds before your competitors do.

Where to start

You don't need to rebuild everything. Start by picking the handful of questions your best customers actually ask, and make your site the clearest, best-structured answer to each one — then make sure your website is technically sound enough for the crawlers to read it. This is exactly the kind of work we fold into our SEO service, and keep current through our website care plans. And if you're weighing AI's role in your marketing more broadly, we've written honestly about where we use AI and where we don't.

Search is being answered, not just listed. The businesses that win the next few years will be the ones the AI trusts enough to name. Better to be that answer than to wonder why the traffic quietly dried up.
